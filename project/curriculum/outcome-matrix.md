# Beginner course outcome and assessment matrix

**Status: working draft, not educator-approved.** This matrix translates the goals in the product plan into observable tasks for the two independently designed tracks. Module and lesson references point to planning positions in `uz-ru.json` and `ru-uz.json`, not to approved instruction. An outline, sample exercise, or passing software test is not evidence that a learner has achieved an outcome. The tasks and proposed evidence below need Russian- and Uzbek-language educator review, accessibility review, recordings with verified rights, and trials with absolute beginners.

The scope is familiar situations with clear, slow speech, short texts, and support appropriate to a beginner. These are project outcomes informed by the plan, not quotations from CEFR descriptors or proof of a certified level. References such as `3.4–3.6` mean module 3, lessons 4 through 6 in the indicated track. A checkpoint should use fresh examples from the material taught, not simply repeat the lesson answers. All checkpoints require a recall item and a listening task, including the script modules; exact rubrics and thresholds remain to be piloted.

## Russian for Uzbek speakers (`uz-ru`)

| Module and planned positions | Observable outcome | Proposed evidence and review focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Cyrillic I (1.1–1.6) | Recognize taught printed letters and read a few familiar syllables and short words left to right. | Match letter forms; select a written syllable after hearing it; read a new combination of taught letters. Review sound–letter explanations and confusion with Latin lookalikes. |
| 2. Cyrillic II (2.1–2.6) | Distinguish further taught letters and signs and recognize stress in familiar examples. | Identify a letter in a short word and select the word heard; include a stress contrast only after reliable audio is reviewed. Check coverage of the full alphabet, soft/hard signs and stress without promising a one-to-one spelling-to-sound mapping. |
| 3. Greetings and identity (3.1–3.6) | Choose an appropriate greeting or farewell and exchange names in a short supported interaction. | Hear an introduction, identify the name, and produce an introduction and a suitable response. Review register, alternative responses and name transliteration. |
| 4. People and things (4.1–4.6) | Identify a familiar person or object and respond to a simple question about it or possession. | Read a short exchange and supply an identification or possession phrase; hear and identify an object. Review pronouns, noun gender and the limits of the taught patterns. |
| 5. Numbers and time (5.1–5.6) | Give an age and understand a taught number, day or simple time in context. | Hear a number or time and select it; write a short age or schedule response. Review number forms, age constructions and time expressions. |
| 6. Family and description (6.1–6.6) | Give a short supported description of family or a familiar person or thing. | Match a spoken description to a person; write a short description using taught vocabulary. Review gender agreement and culturally inclusive family examples. |
| 7. Daily life (7.1–7.6) | Understand and make a few simple statements and questions about familiar routines, including a negative. | Hear a routine and identify an action; write a short routine and answer a simple question. Review person/verb forms, negation and acceptable variants. |
| 8. Food and shopping (8.1–8.6) | Make a polite request and ask or understand a simple price or quantity. | Hear a price and choose it; complete a shop or café exchange using a request. Review case forms used as chunks, price language and register. |
| 9. Places and movement (9.1–9.6) | Ask about a familiar place or destination and understand a short supported response. | Hear a simple direction and select the destination; produce a question appropriate to location or movement. Review `где / куда / откуда` and the taught preposition patterns. |
| 10. Practical interaction (10.1–10.6) | Exchange basic information about home, study or work and write a short message using familiar language. | Read and answer a short message, write a short response, and complete an integrated dialogue with a listening item. Review any past/future chunks and whether the response can be produced without copying a model. |

## Integrated assessment blueprint

Create separate, independently reviewed forms for each track. Sample practice in the application is not this assessment. Each form needs documented task specifications, answer keys and acceptable variants, recordings and transcripts, accessibility alternatives, scoring guidance, and a versioned mapping back to the reviewed lessons and outcomes.

| Area | Proposed task evidence | Limitation and review need |
|---|---|---|
| Listening | Understand a few slow, clearly recorded familiar exchanges, numbers and instructions; answer questions about meaning. | Human-reviewed target-language recordings and transcripts are required. Written alternatives support access but do not establish listening ability. |
| Reading | Interpret short signs, a simple schedule and an everyday message in the target script. | Use fresh, level-appropriate texts and check that questions do not depend on untaught vocabulary. |
| Writing | Give basic personal information and write a brief practical message in response to a prompt. | A fixed answer list cannot cover all valid open responses; define an educator-reviewed rubric and a credible human or validated scoring process. |
| Interaction | Complete a short guided exchange about a familiar need or personal information. | A typed, structured dialogue measures only that task; it does not establish spoken interaction or pronunciation. A speaking component requires a human-reviewed or otherwise appropriately validated method. |

The lesson passing threshold of 80% is provisional; it must not be reused as an assessment standard without evidence. Pilot task difficulty, scoring consistency, accommodations, and learning gains with beginners before setting an assessment decision rule. Report results as an **A1-aligned course assessment** only when the content and assessment have been reviewed; do not call the result an official CEFR certification.

## Review and completion record

For each outcome, the responsible educator should record the reviewed lesson revision, assessment task and version, reviewer and date, audio/rights status, evidence from beginner trials, and unresolved issues. No outcome in this document currently has that evidence attached. The curriculum is not complete until both educators approve coverage, gaps are addressed in published lessons and assessments, and the plan's other lesson and launch gates are met.
